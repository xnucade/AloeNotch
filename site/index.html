Free, without the asterisk.

The other good notch apps are worth their money — they run about $15 to $25. AloeNotch is free in the way that actually matters: no trial that expires, no licence key, no Pro tier holding a feature back, no account to make, and nothing inside the app asking you to upgrade.

It's MIT licensed and the whole thing is on GitHub, so you can read exactly what it does before you run it.

What it doesn't do.

  • No analytics, no telemetry, no account. Nothing about how you use it leaves your Mac.
  • Two network requests, both optional. The weather for your approximate location, and a once-a-day check for a new version. That's the complete list, and either can be switched off.
  • Clipboard history never touches the disk. It lives in memory, clears when the app quits, and skips anything a password manager marks private.
  • The keyboard shortcut needs no Accessibility permission. Only replacing the volume and brightness HUD does, and that's off until you turn it on.

AloeNotch is an independent app, so macOS will ask before the first launch: open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click “Open Anyway”. You only do this once.

Questions

My Mac doesn't have a notch. Does it still work?

Yes. On a display without a notch, AloeNotch draws its own strip in the same place at the top of the screen, and everything behaves the same way.

What permissions does it need?

None, to start with. Calendar and Location are asked for only when you switch those modules on, and Accessibility only if you want the notch to replace the macOS volume and brightness HUD. Everything else — the shelf, the clipboard, the timer, media controls, sound output — needs nothing at all.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

There isn't one. It's MIT licensed and open source, there's no paid tier, and nothing in the app asks you for money. It's a side project, not a business.

How do I update it?

AloeNotch checks GitHub once a day and tells you in the menu bar when something newer exists. Downloading the new .dmg and replacing the app is the whole process — your settings and permissions carry over.

Does it work on an Intel Mac?

Not the current version. 0.6.0 is the last build for Intel Macs and macOS 15, and it still works, but it doesn't get new features or fixes. There's a download link for it above.

Will it slow my Mac down?

It shouldn't. Idle, it sits under one percent of a single core and around 37 MB of memory, and the whole app is about four megabytes on disk.
